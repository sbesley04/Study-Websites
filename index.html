Cross-Cutting Themes for Paragraph Questions

The course is unified by a few big questions. If a paragraph prompt looks unfamiliar, look for these threads:

  • Why did 1989 happen the way it did, and why did the trajectories diverge? Antecedent regime type → transition mode → 1990s outcomes (Modules 10, 11, 12, 13). Pull in Kuran, Way.
  • State-building and democratization under uncertainty. Why these specific institutions (Module 11), how the economy was rebuilt (12), how elites reshuffled (13), how voters and parties formed (14). Pull in Ágh, Lijphart, Bernhard, Deegan-Krause.
  • Nationalism, identity, and the Yugoslav wars. Module 15. Civic vs. ethnic nationalism, Gagnon's strategic-elites thesis, role of subversive institutions and outside powers.
  • How and why democracies decay. Modules 16, 17.1, 17.2, 17.3. Settling accounts, defining backsliding, the Hungarian Frankenstate/mafia state, bottom-up vs. top-down explanations. Pull in Scheppele, Anghel & Jones, Carothers.
  • Europe in a dangerous world. Module 18 (EU's promise + limits as a democracy enforcer), 19.1 (NATO + Russia's revisionism), 19.2 (the war and what to do about it). Pull in Plokhy, Bond.
